For a painting analyse, the most useful framework is to treat **Kingdom of Art, the King of Art, and Cosmic Flowing as overlapping but distinct layers**. A painting is simultaneously a physical object, an individual artistic work, a manifestation of the artist's wider philosophy, part of a much larger oeuvre, and—within the present project—an object that may eventually require institutional-grade cataloguing, provenance, custody, conservation and commercial documentation.

That separation matters because otherwise it is easy either to reduce the works to “acrylic pouring” or, in the opposite direction, to treat the artist's metaphysical language as if it were a scientific description of the materials. Neither is adequate.

## 1. The artist: Cosmo du Mont, the “King of Art”

The source material names the artist **Cosmo du Mont** and describes him not only as a visual artist but as a *Lebens-Künstler*—literally an “artist of life”—and a *Wahrnehmungsforscher*, a researcher/explorer of perception. The texts say that Cosmic Flowing shapes his entire artistic practice, including paintings, sculptures and staged art actions.  The separate *Cosmic Flowing – Wing* document repeats that Cosmic Flowing informs both his visual works and his performed or staged actions.

Within the broader Kingdom of Art project, **“King of Art” is therefore best understood as the artistic persona and cultural identity surrounding the artist**, rather than merely a signature printed on paintings. Earlier project material also connects the identities Martin Mangold, Cosmo du Mont and King of Art. That larger persona is important because the oeuvre has been conceived not just as a body of individual pictures but as part of a much larger artistic world involving Action Art, objects, environments, language, symbols, lifestyle concepts and commercial-cultural manifestations.

This means that a King of Art painting should not automatically be approached as an isolated modern abstract painting whose entire meaning is contained inside its frame.

---

# 2. Cosmic Flowing is the central artistic philosophy

The supplied artist documentation is unusually explicit about this.

It defines **Cosmic Flowing** as the flow of cosmic energy and calls it one of the principal factors in Cosmo du Mont's visual art as well as his “art of living.” The document characterizes it as simultaneously **energetic, mental/spiritual and spiritual**, and connects it with a holistic system developed by the artist.

The artist's own proposition is that humanity exists inside continuously flowing cosmic energies and that those energies can participate in artistic creation. He invokes the conservation of energy as part of his personal explanation for why energy can be used in the creation of art.

For catalogue and art-historical purposes, this needs an important qualification:

**Cosmic Flowing is the artist's philosophical/metaphysical model.**

When we analyse a painting, we can legitimately say:

> “The artist conceptualizes the work through Cosmic Flowing Energy.”

We should not turn that into:

> “The photograph demonstrates the presence of cosmic energy.”

The former documents artistic intention. The latter would make an empirical scientific claim that the image cannot establish.

That distinction will remain important throughout the Kingdom of Art catalogue.

---

# 3. *Panta rhei*: “everything flows”

A particularly useful conceptual key is the artist's explicit use of the Heraclitean formulation:

> **panta rhei — Alles fließt — everything flows.**

The document compares the Flow to an energy network or energy ocean running around and through everything in the universe.

This helps explain why **flow itself**, rather than any particular image or subject, is a plausible unifying element across paintings that look dramatically different.

That conclusion has become progressively clearer from the works examined in this conversation.

No. 7438 presented flow as **radial eruption**.

No. 7542 presented it as **interconnected currents**.

No. 2086 presented it as **diffusion and changing states**.

No. 7693 presented it as **trajectories crossing a structured field**.

No. 97 presented it as **material movement condensing into biomorphic form**.

They do not share one stylistic template. What they appear to share is a concern with **matter in transformation**.

That is a much stronger working definition of Cosmic Flowing Painting than “paintings made by pouring paint.”

---

# 4. The path of least resistance

The original Cosmic Flowing text provides another unusually useful interpretative principle.

It says that Flow, like natural events, follows the **path of least resistance**. The artist compares it explicitly to water travelling toward the sea: water does not struggle to reach its destination; despite obstacles, gravity carries it along a direct natural route.

That philosophical metaphor also gives us a rigorous way to look at the physical paintings.

When we see:

gravity-driven drips, fluid migration, branching streams, displacement around obstacles, spreading, coalescence, narrowing channels, diffusion or viscosity-dependent movement,

those are not merely decorative effects. They can be analysed as the **material analogue of the artist's Flow concept**.

There is therefore a potentially important correspondence:

**conceptual Flow ↔ physical flow of matter.**

But this correspondence should be demonstrated painting by painting rather than assumed.

---

# 5. “Cosmic Flowing Painting” and “Cosmic Flowing colors”

The artist has clarified during this analysis that the works are:

**Cosmic Flowing paintings**

made with:

**“Cosmic Flowing colors = Mixed Media.”**

That is valuable cataloguing information.

For the Kingdom of Art catalogue, I would therefore keep two descriptions simultaneously:

**Artist terminology:**
*Cosmic Flowing colors.*

**Conventional material terminology:**
*Mixed media on canvas*, followed by more precise constituent materials whenever they are scientifically or documentary established.

We should not automatically replace that with “acrylic on canvas” merely because a work resembles acrylic pouring.

Different works we have examined display:

fluid films, thick ridges, cellular structures, glossy passages, metallic or pearlescent appearances, drips, dragged material, combed surfaces, veils and possibly sprayed or blown dispersions.

“Mixed media” is therefore presently the more defensible umbrella description.

---

# 6. Cosmic Flowing is a process language, not a fixed appearance

This is one of the strongest conclusions from the paintings themselves.

The corpus examined so far includes compositions that are:

radial, reticulated, nearly monochrome, atmospheric, aggressively chromatic, highly textured, gravity-driven and biomorphic.

If Cosmic Flowing were merely a recognizable visual style, several of these works would seem to belong to different artists or different series.

If instead Cosmic Flowing describes a **generative system** in which the artist initiates conditions through which colour, matter, force and intervention interact, then their diversity becomes comprehensible.

A useful working formulation for future analysis is:

> **Cosmic Flowing Painting is a process-based artistic practice in which movement, transformation, interaction and the behaviour of materials become both the means of image creation and part of the conceptual subject of the work.**

That is our curatorial synthesis from the works and documents, not a quotation from the artist.

---

# 7. Nature, physical law and artistic agency

A major theme in the broader Kingdom material is the relationship between artistic intention and natural process.

In practical terms, several of the paintings allow gravity, viscosity, surface tension, mixing, diffusion, drying and possibly airflow to determine aspects of the finished image.

The phrase we have repeatedly used—**“nature as co-author”**—is useful for describing this.

But its status should be kept clear. It belongs to the wider Kingdom conceptual vocabulary and our curatorial synthesis; it is not simply another way of saying that the artist has relinquished authorship.

The artist still selects:

material, colour, quantity, timing, orientation, sequence, intervention and stopping point.

Natural forces then contribute outcomes that cannot necessarily be microscopically predetermined.

The authorship therefore resembles a **negotiation between intentional action and emergent material behaviour**.

That is one reason we have been interested in distinguishing gestures that are painted directly from traces produced by gravity, flow, lacing, spreading or displacement.

---

# 8. Balance-Chance: an important correction

We should refine something from the earlier painting analyses.

I occasionally used **“Balance-Chance”** as a convenient expression for a balance between artistic control and chance.

That is an interesting curatorial metaphor, but it is **not the literal meaning given to the term in the supplied Cosmic Flowing documentation**.

The Wing document defines *Balance-Chance* as giving body, mind and soul an inspiring **chance to attain beneficial balance**.

So going forward I would make this distinction:

| Term                 | Proper status in the framework                                                     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Balance-Chance**   | Artist's holistic/life-art concept concerning the opportunity for balance          |
| **control × chance** | Our analytical description of intentional and emergent processes in some paintings |

They may conceptually resonate with each other, but we should not collapse them into the same definition.

---

# 9. Lebens-Kunst: the artwork extends beyond the canvas

Cosmic Flowing was not conceived only as a painting technique.

The documents explicitly extend it into **Lebens-Kunst**, an “art of living.”

The Cosmic Flowing Wing is particularly informative. It was conceived as a multisensory experiential space incorporating **sound, colour, light, space, warmth, scent and vibration**.  The text further describes three-dimensional sound, bodily vibration, monochromatic blue and the dissolution of ordinary spatial perception as part of an integrated sensory experience.

This provides strong evidence that Cosmo du Mont's artistic ambition is closer to a **Gesamtkunstwerk—an integrated total-art environment—than simply to conventional easel painting**.

That observation is highly relevant to Kingdom of Art.

Kingdom of Art can therefore contain:

painting, sculpture, Action Art, installations, immersive spaces, artistic personas, objects, rituals, performance, storytelling, media and lifestyle design,

without those activities necessarily becoming detached from the core artistic practice.

The connective tissue is the artistic world around Cosmic Flowing and the King of Art persona.

---

# 10. Spiritual and wellness material: context, not evidence

Some historical Cosmic Flowing material goes much further into wellness, spiritual healing and bodily effects. The Wing document itself carefully states that Cosmo du Mont is not a doctor or medical practitioner and elsewhere describes the practices as spiritual/self-help rather than medical treatment.  It also explicitly says that its coaches do not diagnose and that a “Balance-Chance,” rather than a cure, is promised.

For the art project, the appropriate treatment is therefore:

**document these claims as part of the historical artistic/spiritual worldview, but do not use them as scientifically established properties of a painting.**

A Cosmic Flowing painting can legitimately be catalogued as emerging from that philosophy.

It should not be catalogued as medically therapeutic without separate evidence that would support such a statement.

---

# 11. Action Art and Cosmic Flowing Blowing

The broader Kingdom material also establishes a significant **Action Art** dimension.

Projects developed under personas/concepts including **Harry Cane** and **Thor Nado** explored wind, weather and outdoor painting processes, and were subsequently integrated conceptually into **Cosmic Flowing Blowing** / “Wind made Art.”

That becomes important when inspecting an individual work.

A painting may visibly appear to involve blowing or air dispersion, but visual similarity alone is not enough to classify it as *Cosmic Flowing Blowing*.

The correct catalogue process is:

> observable blown-looking morphology → technical hypothesis → artist/studio confirmation → series/technique classification.

That distinction became relevant in our discussion of no. 2086.

# 12. The seven-element signature

Another established Kingdom of Art framework is the seven-element vocabulary:

| Element    | Broad interpretative domain                                         |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fire**   | energy, heat, transformation, ignition                              |
| **Earth**  | matter, substance, grounding, mineral/material presence             |
| **Water**  | flow, fluidity, adaptation, continuity                              |
| **Air**    | movement, breath, wind, invisible forces                            |
| **Light**  | illumination, perception, colour, revelation                        |
| **Nature** | generative processes, organic structure, life's ordering principles |
| **Cosmos** | larger order, connectedness and harmonious structure                |

An important nuance previously established in the project is that **Cosmos is not simply synonymous with Universe**. *Universe* describes the totality; *Cosmos* emphasizes **ordered and meaningful harmony**.

This gives the seven elements a coherent place inside the artistic system.

They should nevertheless be used as an **interpretative vocabulary**, not as a scavenger hunt in which every painting must contain seven visibly separate symbols.

For example, a gravity-driven yellow stream might resonate simultaneously with Water, Light and Fire depending on context.

The elements are better understood as interacting qualities.

---

# 13. “Cosmogony of Colors”

Another important Kingdom idea is the **Kosmogonie der Farben—Cosmogony of Colors**.

In that framework, colour is not merely used to fill predefined shapes.

Colours themselves behave almost as **actors or entities**:

they meet, repel, overlap, dissolve, contaminate, dominate, disappear and re-emerge.

That concept is extremely useful when looking at 7438 and 7542.

Instead of asking:

> “What does the red represent?”

we can ask:

> “What does the red *do* relative to blue, black, white and gold?”

That shifts the analysis from elementary colour symbolism toward **relationships and behaviour**.

It also fits Cosmic Flowing remarkably well because colour becomes another moving participant in the generative system.

---

# 14. Concrete Abstraction

The broader artistic vocabulary also includes **Konkrete Abstraktion—Concrete Abstraction**.

Here “concrete” is useful in the sense that although the image may be nonfigurative, the paint and material event are emphatically **real physical occurrences**.

A drip is not an illustration of a drip.

It *is* a drip.

A ridge is not an image of displacement.

It is displaced matter.

The conceptual content can therefore arise directly out of the actual physical process.

That provides a particularly productive lens for Cosmic Flowing Painting.

---

# 15. Chaos → harmony

Another repeated Kingdom idea is the movement from **chaos toward harmony or ordered form**.

This should not be interpreted as every painting starting ugly and becoming beautiful.

A more interesting interpretation is multiscale.

At a small scale we may see:

irregular droplets, turbulent boundaries, cells, splits, deformations and unpredictable collisions.

At a larger scale those events can form:

balanced fields, coherent vectors, vortices, networks, organisms or stable compositions.

This was especially apparent in nos. 7542 and 97.

The compelling feature is:

> **local unpredictability can create global organization.**

That is also one of the reasons the concept of emergence has become useful in our analysis.

---

# 16. Perfect Imperfection

The Kingdom material also develops the idea of **Perfect Imperfection**, including the associated concept of works or archetypes bearing *Blessuren*—marks, wounds or scars.

That concept has an important consequence for object analysis.

An apparent irregularity must not automatically be classified as damage.

A drip on the edge, a scar-like mark, exposed material, an asymmetrical boundary or a deformation may be:

an execution feature,

a deliberate aesthetic element,

a later accidental mark,

or genuine deterioration.

The catalogue must distinguish these.

This is particularly relevant for Cosmic Flowing works because apparent “imperfection” frequently records the physical history of their creation.

---

# 17. Archetypes, originals and the Kingdom object system

The Kingdom of Art project also uses **Archetype**, original and edition/reproduction concepts as part of its artistic and commercial vocabulary.

That becomes relevant when cataloguing because “unique-looking object” and “unique work” are not necessarily equivalent propositions.

For every object we eventually need to determine separately:

physical uniqueness,

artist classification,

edition status,

prototype/archetype status,

reproduction status,

copyright status,

and ownership.

This becomes particularly important for concepts such as the **GoldFann Artbox**, where an artwork, archetypal image, one-of-one related object, publication and certificate can deliberately form a larger artistic bundle.

So future image analysis should not infer edition status from appearance.

# 18. Kingdom of Art is larger than the artist's current production

The modern **Kingdom of Art project** takes these artistic ideas and attempts to turn them into an enduring cultural and institutional structure around the artist.

The strategic work carried out in the project has therefore gone well beyond selling individual pictures. It addresses:

artist legacy,

collection management,

museum-quality cataloguing,

intellectual property,

media production,

social recognition,

exhibitions and events,

products and editions,

experiential art,

licensing,

commercial activities,

succession,

and preservation of the artistic doctrine.

The long-term goal is closer to building an **institutional artist estate and cultural universe** than operating an ordinary gallery or artist studio.

That is why rigorous analysis of hundreds or ultimately many thousands of works matters. Their relationships may reveal periods, families, techniques and conceptual developments that are invisible when each work is viewed independently.

# 19. The analysis framework I would use from now on

For every new painting, I suggest we consistently analyse it through eight layers:

1. **Object facts:** front/reverse, visible number, dimensions, support, markings, construction and condition observations.
2. **Material analysis:** Cosmic Flowing colors/mixed media, surface morphology, viscosity evidence, tool traces, flow mechanisms and technical hypotheses.
3. **Formal analysis:** colour distribution, topology, focal structure, vectors, negative space, scale and composition.
4. **Artist classification:** confirmed Cosmic Flowing Painting, Cosmic Flowing Blowing or another King of Art family only when supported.
5. **Kingdom interpretation:** panta rhei, Cosmic Flowing, seven elements, Cosmogony of Colors, Perfect Imperfection, nature/material agency, chaos→harmony and related concepts where genuinely relevant.
6. **Independent curatorial interpretation:** field/event, emergence, diffusion, eruption, trajectory, biomorphism, representational threshold and art-historical comparisons. These are analytical tools, not artist-authored terminology.
7. **Corpus relationship:** compare the work with other documented King of Art works to identify families, transitions, repeated processes and possible chronological tendencies.
8. **Institutional record:** stable asset ID, studio number, provenance, rights, condition grade, fingerprint, valuation status and catalogue confidence.

That approach avoids forcing the Kingdom philosophy onto the image while also avoiding the opposite mistake of analysing a Cosmic Flowing painting as though we knew nothing about the conceptual system in which the artist created it.

## The most important conceptual distinction

Going forward, I would explicitly label information in every catalogue analysis as belonging to one of three epistemic classes:

> **Artist statement:** what Cosmo du Mont / the King of Art says the work or technique means.

> **Observable fact:** what can actually be established from the physical work or photographic evidence.

> **Curatorial interpretation:** what we infer by placing those observations within the Kingdom of Art philosophy, the wider corpus and art history.

That distinction is particularly important for Cosmic Flowing because the artist intentionally merges **matter, spirituality, perception, cosmology and life philosophy**.

Handled carefully, that is not a weakness of the catalogue. It is what allows us to preserve the artist's distinctive worldview while simultaneously creating a rigorous, defensible analytical record of the paintings themselves.
